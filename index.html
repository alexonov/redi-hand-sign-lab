Hand Sign Lab

Teach your laptop your hand signs with two classic models: logistic regression and a decision tree.

● recording
The camera image stays on your laptop. Nothing is uploaded.
0.50

The model only acts when its top probability is above the cutoff. Otherwise it says "not sure". This model and cutoff are used everywhere: live guess, test, game.

Train a model (step 2) to see live predictions here.

1 Collect training data

Hold the button (or keys 1 2 3) while you show the sign. Move your hand around a bit: closer, further, tilted. Aim for 100+ examples per sign. You can rename the signs.

Load a teammate's CSV to test your model on their hand: its training rows go into your test set.

2 Train and compare

Pick what the models see, then train. The tree retrains as you move its sliders.

3 Test: new data the models never saw

Record a few seconds per sign in a different way: other hand, other distance, stand up, ask someone else. These examples are never used for training, only for grading.

4 Play: rock, paper, scissors

Uses the model and cutoff chosen under the camera. Throw your sign on "shoot!". The model watches for half a second and takes the sign it saw most. If it is "not sure" most of that time, you lose the round. Signs must be named Rock, Paper, Scissors in this order.
